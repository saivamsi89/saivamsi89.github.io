A working prototype is an important milestone, but it is not the same as a manufacturable product.
Decisions made early in the design cycle have a large effect on cost, yield and time-to-market once
you scale. Here are six areas worth getting right from the start.

## 1. Lock down requirements before layout

Electrical, mechanical, environmental and regulatory requirements all shape the design. Changing
an operating temperature range or enclosure size after layout often means starting the board again.

## 2. Choose components with availability in mind

A part that is easy to buy in tens may be hard to source in thousands. Prefer components with multiple
sources and an active lifecycle status, and identify alternates for critical parts early.

## 3. Design for manufacturability (DFM)

Follow your fabricator's capabilities for trace width, spacing, via sizes and layer stack-up. Keep
component spacing and orientation assembly-friendly, and add fiducials and tooling holes.

## 4. Design for testability (DFT)

Plan how every production board will be tested. Accessible test points, programming headers and a
defined functional test procedure save significant time on the production line.

## 5. Treat firmware as part of the product

Firmware needs version control, a reliable programming method for production, and ideally a safe
update path in the field. Bring firmware and hardware teams together from the beginning.

## 6. Validate before you scale

Run a small pilot batch before committing to volume. It reveals assembly issues, test gaps and
component variations that a single hand-built prototype will never show.
